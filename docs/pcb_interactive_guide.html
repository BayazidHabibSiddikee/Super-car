Mezzanine Stack Overview

How the Double-Decker Stack Works

Click any PCB part ⬅

Because your board is super compact (46 × 32 mm), it uses a 2-Story Mezzanine Stack on the Top (`F.Cu`) side so nothing hangs underneath to hit your 2S 18650 battery:

FLOOR 1
Surface-Mount Chips (`U2`, `U3`, `D2`, `R1–R3`, `Q1–Q2`): Solder these directly onto the PCB pads first. They are only 1.6 mm tall!
FLOOR 2
Tall Female Headers (`JESP` & `JJQ`): Solder four 1×8 Female Pin Headers (`8.5 mm` tall) into the outer rows. The **ESP32-CAM** and **JQ6500-16P** plug into these headers like a bridge **6.5 mm above** `U2` and `U3`!

💡 Try it now: Click the "Stacked Modules (3D Overlay)" button above the board to see how the ESP32-CAM and JQ6500 sit on top of the SMD chips, or click any component on the PCB for exact Pin-1 orientation!

KiCad DRC Check

Why Your Bottom Bar Says `Unrouted: 20`

Look at the bottom-left corner of your screenshot: Pads: 98 | Vias: 2 | Track Segments: 120 | Unrouted: 20.

Why are there 20 unrouted connections?

All 20 of those unrouted airwires are GND (Ground) pins! When KiCad opens a newly generated `.kicad_pcb` file, it leaves copper pour zones unfilled until you press the fill hotkey.

✅ The 1-Second Fix in KiCad:
  1. Click anywhere inside the KiCad PCB Editor window (not the 3D Viewer).
  2. Press the B key on your keyboard.
  3. KiCad immediately floods the `F.Cu` and `B.Cu` ground zones and connects all 20 `GND` pads with thermal-relief spokes!
  4. Look at the bottom bar again: Unrouted: 0!
  5. Go to Inspect → Design Rules Checker → Run DRC to verify your board is 100% ready to print or order.
